# LINX — THE CAMPAIGN BOOK
### Thirty concepts. Ten pain, ten aspiration, ten pattern interrupt.

Read [`ART-DIRECTION.md`](ART-DIRECTION.md) first. It contains the rules every
concept below obeys.

Concepts marked **▣ BUILT** are designed and rendered in [`../out/campaign/`](../out/campaign/).

## 01 · DEADHEAD ▣ BUILT

**Core idea** — The word every carrier hates, made into the object it describes.

**Psychological trigger** — Recognition, then arithmetic. Deadhead is the one loss
every owner-operator can feel but has never seen quantified.

**Visual** — The word `DEADHEAD` set enormous, condensed, filling the frame edge to
edge. The letterforms are *hollow* — outlined only, empty inside — except the final
letter, which is solid. Beneath, a hairline rule and a single line of data: the
percentage of empty miles and what it costs per year. Nothing else. The emptiness
inside the type *is* the empty trailer.

**Art direction** — Album cover. Joy Division / Peter Saville restraint. The type is
the entire image; there is no illustration, no photograph, no ornament. Absolute
edge-to-edge confidence, tight optical letterspacing, one hairline.

**Lighting** — None. Flat. This is print, not photography.

**Palette** — Bone white `#EDEAE3` on ink `#0B0B0C`. One accent: signal red `#D93A2B`
on the solid letter only.

**Typography** — Heavy condensed grotesk, uppercase, tracked tight to touching.
Micro-caps mono for the data line.

**Headline** — DEADHEAD

**Subheadline** — The miles you pay for twice.

**Caption** —
> Nobody bills you for an empty trailer. That's why it's the most expensive thing
> you own.
>
> A quarter of your miles running empty means you need a third more on the loaded
> ones just to break even. Most carriers have never run that number. The ones who
> have don't book their own freight anymore.

**CTA** — Free Dispatch Fit Review → linxdispatch.com

**Why it works** — It's a vocabulary word only the audience knows. Outsiders scroll
past confused; insiders stop cold. That's the perfect filter for a paid feed.

**Image prompt** — *Typographic only — no generation required.*

**Animation** — Letters fill with red one at a time, left to right, then drain.
**Carousel** — 1: DEADHEAD. 2: the arithmetic. 3: what a planned backhaul looks like.
**Story** — Same lockup, 9:16, data line becomes a tap target.
**Reel thumb** — Just the word, cropped so `DEADH` fills the frame.

## 02 · SIDE EFFECTS ▣ BUILT

**Core idea** — Dispatch as a prescription drug, with the side effects being the
benefits.

**Psychological trigger** — Subversion. The reader braces for a warning and gets a
promise. The joke lands before the pitch does.

**Visual** — A pharmaceutical package insert, reproduced exactly: clinical cream
stock, a ruled dosage table, a black warning bar, the folded-paper crease running
vertically. Product name `DISPATCH™`. Dosage `1 carrier, daily`. Then the side effects
list, deadpan: *sleeping through the night · answering forty fewer calls a week ·
sudden loss of interest in load boards · your kid recognising your voice on the phone.*

**Art direction** — Medicine ad, 1990s. Absolutely no design flair — the power is in
how boring it looks. Every element is a real insert element: NDC number, warning
triangle, "Rx only", a barcode.

**Lighting** — Flat scan. Slight paper grain.

**Palette** — Insert cream `#F4F1E8`, ink black, one warning red `#C3271E`.

**Typography** — Condensed pharma serif for the product name; 8–10pt grotesk for the
body, set justified in narrow columns, deliberately dense.

**Headline** — DISPATCH™

**Subheadline** — Side effects may include:

**Caption** —
> Ask your dispatcher if getting your week back is right for you.
>
> Common side effects include sleeping through the night, taking fewer calls, and
> forgetting what a load board looks like. In rare cases, patients report enjoying
> the business again.

**CTA** — Free Dispatch Fit Review → linxdispatch.com

**Why it works** — Direct lift of the reference's strongest device. The genre is so
recognisable that the frame does the stopping and the copy does the selling.

**Image prompt** — *Typographic only.*

**Animation** — The insert unfolds along its creases.
**Carousel** — Each panel of the insert is a slide.
**Story** — Crop to the side-effects list alone.
**Reel thumb** — The black warning bar, full bleed, three words visible.

## 03 · THE FREEDOM TAX ▣ BUILT

**Core idea** — What you actually pay for the privilege of "being your own boss."

**Psychological trigger** — Loss aversion, made official. A bill you didn't know you
were paying is worse than a price you chose.

**Visual** — A wartime government notice. Two-colour letterpress on kraft stock,
visible ink misregistration, a heavy rule border. Headline `THE FREEDOM TAX` in
condensed slab. A ruled schedule of what is owed — hours, dollars, weekends — each
line rendered like a levy. Bottom: an official-looking authority line and a serial
number. It should look like it was found in an archive.

**Art direction** — Propaganda poster / public notice, 1943. Letterpress texture,
knocked-out type, no photography. Slight foxing on the paper.

**Lighting** — None. Print artifact.

**Palette** — Kraft `#D8CDB4`, oxblood `#7A2318`, ink black. Three colours, held.

**Typography** — Condensed slab for the headline, wood-type numerals for the schedule,
tiny caps for the authority line.

**Headline** — THE FREEDOM TAX

**Subheadline** — Payable weekly. In hours.

**Caption** —
> You left to stop working for someone else. Now you work for every broker with your
> number.
>
> The freedom tax isn't money. It's the 11pm phone calls, the Sunday spent on a load
> board, the rate you took because you were too tired to argue. It's assessed weekly
> and nobody sends you the bill.

**CTA** — Free Dispatch Fit Review → linxdispatch.com

**Why it works** — The genre carries authority and age. It reads as a document rather
than an ad, which buys three extra seconds of attention.

**Image prompt** — *Typographic only.*

**Animation** — Serial number ticks upward; the paper takes a slow curl.
**Carousel** — Each levy line as its own notice.
**Story** — The schedule alone, scrolling.
**Reel thumb** — `THE FREEDOM TAX` cropped to fill.

## 04 · THE BOTTLENECK

**Core idea** — One truck. One driver. One phone. Everything the business could be,
waiting behind a single point of failure — and that point of failure is you.

**Psychological trigger** — Identity threat, delivered gently. Not "you're bad at
this" but "you're the constraint, and that's a compliment you can't afford."

**Visual** — A single glass bottleneck, shot macro against black, with a slow river
of amber grain suspended mid-fall inside the neck. Everything above the neck is dense
with grain; below, almost empty. Extremely shallow depth of field. The glass has a
hairline highlight and nothing else.

**Art direction** — Luxury product photography. Think a Hennessy or Nars campaign —
one object, black field, obsessive lighting. No context, no scale reference.

**Lighting** — Single hard rim light from camera left, deep falloff, one specular
highlight running the length of the glass.

**Palette** — Black `#08080A`, amber grain `#C98B2E`, one cold highlight `#E8EEF2`.

**Typography** — Very light modern serif, small, bottom-left. Whispered.

**Headline** — Everything moves at the speed of one person.

**Subheadline** — Yours.

**Caption** —
> Your truck isn't the constraint. Your trailer isn't the constraint.
>
> The constraint is that every load, every rate, every packet and every check call
> has to pass through one person who is also driving.

**CTA** — Free Dispatch Fit Review → linxdispatch.com

**Why it works** — It flatters and indicts in the same breath. Owner-operators are
proud of doing everything; this reframes that pride as the ceiling.

**Image prompt** — *Macro product photograph of a single clear glass bottleneck against
pure black, amber sand suspended mid-fall inside the narrow neck, dense above and
sparse below, extreme shallow depth of field, single hard rim light from camera left,
one long specular highlight on the glass, no label, no background, luxury spirits
campaign photography, shot on 100mm macro, f/2.8 --ar 4:5 --style raw*

**Animation** — The grain falls one grain at a time, impossibly slowly.
**Carousel** — Slide 1 the bottleneck; 2–4 what passes through it, listed.
**Story** — Vertical crop on the neck, grain falling.
**Reel thumb** — The neck only, filling frame.

## 05 · THE 11TH HOUR

**Core idea** — Every bad rate in trucking was agreed to after 10pm.

**Psychological trigger** — Specific recognition. Not "you're tired" — *"you took that
load at 11:47 and you know exactly which one I mean."*

**Visual** — A truck stop at night shot from a great distance and from behind: a
single sodium-lit lot, thirty trailers, and exactly one cab with its interior light
on. Wide, cold, almost aerial. The lit cab is tiny in the frame — maybe 2% of it. The
rest is dark asphalt and orange lamp bloom.

**Art direction** — Cinematic wide, Roger Deakins. Scale as emotion — the smallness of
the lit window carries the entire idea. No people visible.

**Lighting** — Sodium vapour, heavy orange bloom, deep blue-black shadows. Practical
light only.

**Palette** — Sodium orange `#E08A2C`, night blue `#0D1622`, black.

**Typography** — Small mono, top-left, timestamp format. Almost a data burn-in.

**Headline** — 23:47

**Subheadline** — The most expensive hour in trucking.

**Caption** —
> Fatigue is a pricing strategy. It just isn't yours.
>
> By the time you've been up seventeen hours, "fine, I'll take it" costs less than
> one more phone call. Brokers know the math. That's why they call late.

**CTA** — Free Dispatch Fit Review → linxdispatch.com

**Why it works** — The image is beautiful enough to stop a non-trucker, and specific
enough to gut a trucker. Both audiences stop, for different reasons.

**Image prompt** — *Wide aerial night photograph of a truck stop parking lot, thirty
parked semi trailers in rows, sodium vapour lamps with heavy orange bloom, exactly one
truck cab with a warm interior light on, everything else dark, wet asphalt reflections,
cold blue-black shadows, cinematic wide shot, Roger Deakins lighting, anamorphic,
shot on ARRI Alexa --ar 4:5 --style raw*

**Animation** — Every other light in the lot goes out. The one cab stays lit.
**Carousel** — The night's log, hour by hour.
**Story** — Slow push toward the lit window.
**Reel thumb** — Tight crop on the single lit cab, grain heavy.

## 06 · THAT'S THE BEST I CAN DO

**Core idea** — The six most expensive words a carrier ever believes.

**Psychological trigger** — Villain identification. Not anger at brokers — anger at
being outgunned, which is fixable.

**Visual** — A courtroom-style transcript page, monospace, double-spaced, with line
numbers down the left margin. Most of the page is greyed out. One exchange is
rendered in full black. The final line — the broker's — is highlighted. Below, a
second transcript fragment from a different call, same broker, four hundred and forty
dollars higher.

**Art direction** — Legal document. Court reporter's transcript, exactly. Line
numbers, certification stamp, page number. No design intervention whatsoever.

**Lighting** — Flat document scan, slight paper warmth.

**Palette** — Document white `#FAF8F3`, black, one highlighter yellow `#F2D64B`.

**Typography** — Courier. Only Courier. The genre demands it.

**Headline** — *(none — the transcript is the headline)*

**Subheadline** — EXHIBIT A.

**Caption** —
> "That's the best I can do."
>
> It wasn't. It was the best he had to do — because the person on the other end was
> driving, and had forty seconds, and no rate history in front of him.
>
> Same broker. Same lane. $440 more, once someone who does this full time picked up.

**CTA** — Free Dispatch Fit Review → linxdispatch.com

**Why it works** — Documentary evidence is the most persuasive format that exists.
Presenting it as an exhibit rather than a claim changes how the brain files it.

**Image prompt** — *Typographic only.*

**Animation** — The highlighter sweeps across the final line.
**Carousel** — Page 1 the first call, page 2 the second, page 3 the delta.
**Story** — The highlighted line alone.
**Reel thumb** — Courier text, one line highlighted, cropped tight.

## 07 · PAPERWORK

**Core idea** — The physical volume of admin one truck generates in a year, shown at
actual scale.

**Psychological trigger** — Scale shock. Everyone underestimates it until they see it
stacked.

**Visual** — A single column of paper — rate confirmations, BOLs, carrier packets —
stacked to roughly the height of a person, standing alone in an empty white room. Shot
straight on, slightly wide, so the room's emptiness sells the height. The stack leans
very slightly. No hands, no desk, no office.

**Art direction** — Conceptual still life, gallery installation. Think Wolfgang
Tillmans or an Apple environmental shot: one object, one room, perfect light.

**Lighting** — Enormous soft north-facing window light from camera left. Long soft
shadow across a pale concrete floor.

**Palette** — Gallery white `#F2F2EF`, paper cream, one long grey shadow.

**Typography** — Tiny gallery wall label, bottom-left, exactly as a museum would set
it: title, medium, dimensions, year.

**Headline** — *Untitled (One Truck, One Year)*

**Subheadline** — Rate confirmations, bills of lading, carrier packets. 2026.

**Caption** —
> One truck generates about four feet of paper a year.
>
> None of it moves freight. All of it has to happen. The only real question is whose
> evening it happens in.

**CTA** — Free Dispatch Fit Review → linxdispatch.com

**Why it works** — The museum label is the whole joke and the whole craft. It elevates
a mundane grievance into an object worth looking at.

**Image prompt** — *A single tall stack of paper documents approximately five feet
high standing alone on a pale polished concrete floor in an empty white gallery room,
slight lean, enormous soft north window light from camera left, long soft shadow, no
people, no furniture, conceptual still life, gallery installation photography,
shot on Hasselblad, f/8 --ar 4:5 --style raw*

**Animation** — The stack grows sheet by sheet, accelerating.
**Carousel** — Each document type as its own gallery plate.
**Story** — Tilt up the full height of the stack.
**Reel thumb** — Stack top against white, dramatic negative space.

**Headline** — THE LOAD BOARD

## 10 · THE MARGIN ▣ BUILT

**Core idea** — A typographic joke about the thing that decides whether the business
survives.

**Psychological trigger** — Intelligence flattery. The viewer gets it in one beat and
feels clever.

**Visual** — A page from an architecture monograph. A vast, absurd, luxurious margin —
easily 80% of the page — and in the bottom-right corner, a single small block of
justified text about how thin the actual margin is. Faint blue non-repro grid lines,
crop marks, a folio number. The negative space *is* the margin, and it's the only
generous margin in the business.

**Art direction** — Swiss book design. Josef Müller-Brockmann, Lars Müller Publishers.
Ruthless, mathematical, expensive-looking emptiness.

**Lighting** — None. Pure print object.

**Palette** — Warm paper `#F5F3EC`, ink, one non-repro blue `#B8CDE0` for the grid.

**Typography** — Neue Haas Grotesk-class. 9pt. Justified. Nothing large anywhere.

**Headline** — *(none — the smallest type on the page is the headline)*

**Subheadline** — margin, n.

**Caption** —
> Everything on this page is margin except the part that matters.
>
> That's also a description of most owner-operator P&Ls. The difference between a
> $1.80 lane and a $2.40 lane isn't effort. It's whether anyone was negotiating.

**CTA** — Free Dispatch Fit Review → linxdispatch.com

**Why it works** — It is the single most art-directed idea in the set and the one most
likely to be reposted by designers, which buys reach the media budget doesn't pay for.

**Image prompt** — *Typographic only.*

**Animation** — The text block shrinks a little more each second.
**Carousel** — The margin narrows slide by slide until the text is crushed.
**Story** — Same page, vertical, text block bottom-right.
**Reel thumb** — Almost empty frame. Highest curiosity gap in the set.

## 11 · BOOKED ▣ BUILT

**Core idea** — A calendar as a minimalist art object. Every day solid. Weekends
empty on purpose.

**Psychological trigger** — Certainty as luxury. The fantasy isn't more work — it's
knowing.

**Visual** — A 7×5 grid filling most of the frame. Weekdays are solid filled blocks;
Saturday and Sunday are empty outlines. No numbers, no month, no labels except a
whispered `BOOKED` and a date range in micro type. The pattern reads instantly, from
across a room, at thumbnail size.

**Art direction** — Swiss / Apple product launch. Grid, silence, one word. The kind of
image that works at 40px because it's a *pattern*, not a picture.

**Lighting** — None. Flat graphic.

**Palette** — Off-white `#F4F2ED`, ink `#111114`, one filled colour — deep green
`#1F4D3D`. Weekend outlines in ink only.

**Typography** — Grotesk, small, wide-tracked. Nothing above 24px.

**Headline** — BOOKED

**Subheadline** — Weekdays covered. Weekends deliberate.

**Caption** —
> The goal was never a full calendar. It was a *known* one.
>
> Five days planned around your lanes and your home time. Two days that stay empty
> because you decided they would.

**CTA** — Free Dispatch Fit Review → linxdispatch.com
**Why it works** — Legible at thumbnail, which is where the feed war is won. And it
sells the weekend, not the work — the only aspiration that actually converts.
**Image prompt** — *Typographic only.*
**Animation** — Blocks fill one by one, Monday to Friday, then stop.
**Carousel** — Four weeks, filling. **Story** — Vertical 5×7. **Reel thumb** — The grid alone.

## 15 · THE INVISIBLE EMPLOYEE ▣ BUILT

**Core idea** — Issue the dispatcher an employee record. Hire someone whose salary
line is smaller than what they recover.

**Psychological trigger** — Reframe from cost to headcount. A percentage feels like a
loss; an employee feels like an asset.

**Visual** — A personnel file card, reproduced exactly: a manila-stock record with a
punched hole, ruled fields, a typewritten record, an ink date stamp rotated slightly
off-axis, and — where the photograph should be — an empty silhouette box. Fields:
`POSITION: Dispatch` · `HOURS: Before yours` · `REPORTS TO: You` · `SALARY: Less than
the rates recovered`.

**Art direction** — Institutional document, 1970s HR. Typewriter impression, mis-fed
lines, a real rubber stamp. No design, only accurate reproduction.

**Lighting** — Flat scan. Paper tooth visible.

**Palette** — Manila `#E4D5B7`, typewriter black, stamp red `#B23A2E`, one blue biro.

**Typography** — Typewriter face only. Slight character-weight variation, as a real
platen produces.

**Headline** — PERSONNEL RECORD

**Subheadline** — Employee No. 002. Never seen. Always working.

**Caption** —
> Most carriers think of dispatch as a percentage. It's cheaper to think of it as a
> hire.
