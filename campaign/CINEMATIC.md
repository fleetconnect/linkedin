## THE PHOTOGRAPHY BIBLE

Every truck in this campaign is lit like a $400,000 car, not documented like a work
vehicle. That distinction is the entire brand.

**Camera** — Low. Almost always below the beltline, often at tyre height. A truck shot
from eye level looks like a listing photo; shot from 30 inches it looks like a
monument. Three-quarter front for hero beauty, dead-on rear for menace, extreme wide
for scale, macro for craft.

**Glass** — Anamorphic primes. The horizontal flare and oval bokeh are half of why
Volvo Trucks films feel expensive. Specify `35mm anamorphic` or `50mm anamorphic`.

**Light** — One dominant source, hard, raking down the length of the vehicle so the
body line reads as a single unbroken highlight. Deep controlled shadow everywhere else.
Never flat. Never on-camera fill.

**Hour** — Blue hour and the ten minutes after sunset, overwhelmingly. Golden hour for
the aspiration bucket only. Harsh noon never.

**Weather** — Wet ground is non-negotiable on at least half the set. Reflection doubles
the subject and buys production value for free. Fog, dust, diesel haze, snow spindrift.

**Retouching** — Product-grade. Chrome must have real environment reflection, paint
must have depth, tyres must have sidewall texture. The tell of a cheap render is flat
chrome and plastic rubber.

**Forbidden** — Motion blur clichés, lens dirt overlays, HDR halos, fisheye, any driver
smiling at camera, thumbs up, headsets, clipboards, stock-photo staging.

### The negative prompt (append to every generation)

> `no text, no lettering, no watermark, no logos, no brand badges, no license plate text,
> no people looking at camera, no thumbs up, no headsets, no clipboards, no fisheye,
> no HDR halo, no oversaturation, no plastic-looking rubber, no flat chrome, no
> stock-photo staging, no motion blur streaks`

### The layout system

Every cinematic piece uses one of three lockups. All three are built and live in
`build/campaign/_cinematic.css`:

- **LOWER THIRD** — image full bleed, a scrim from `rgba(0,0,0,0)` to `rgba(0,0,0,.88)`
  across the bottom 38%, headline in condensed caps sitting on it, credit block beneath.
- **CORNER PLATE** — image full bleed, one small solid plate in a corner carrying the
  headline. Maximum image, minimum interruption. The Porsche move.
- **LETTERBOX** — image occupies the centre 62%, hard bands of flat colour above and
  below. Headline in the lower band. The A24 move.

Safe area is 84px on all sides. The hero vehicle must never sit under the scrim.

# PRODUCTION NOTES

## Generating the hero images

Three routes, in order of quality:

1. **Midjourney v7** — paste the prompt, append the negative prompt block, add
   `--ar 4:5 --style raw --stylize 250`. Best chrome and paint rendering of the three.
2. **GPT Image / Nano Banana** — paste the prompt verbatim. Better at composition
   adherence, slightly weaker on metal.
3. **beehiiv (in this workspace)** — the `generate_image` tool is wired to Gemini and
   would run these directly. It is currently blocked behind a per-call approval that
   did not clear in this session; approve the tool in an interactive session and the
   set can be generated end to end.

Generate at 4:5. Composite into the layout system in `build/campaign/`, then render
with `./build/render-campaign.sh`.

## Casting the truck

Vary the iron across the set, deliberately. A feed of twenty-four Cascadias reads as
one fleet's marketing. Spread across: Peterbilt 389 (long hood, chrome, aspiration),
Kenworth W900 (classic, owner-operator identity), Freightliner Cascadia (modern,
blacked-out, volume), Western Star 49X (heavy, severe-duty), Mack Anthem, Volvo VNL.
Match the truck to the emotion — a 389 for pride, a Cascadia for efficiency.

## Claims discipline

Unchanged from the book. Only `+$440 per load`, `100+ carriers` and
`$2,000–$4,000 per month` are the client's published figures. Nothing else numeric
goes on a creative without a source.
